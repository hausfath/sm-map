# Literature and data scan: surficial mineralization feedstocks, rates, costs

Produced 2026-09-08 by a web-research subagent (Sonnet) for the SM Atlas plan.
Every figure carries a verification status. **UNVERIFIED** means the number came
from an abstract, a secondary summary, or search-result text and must be checked
against the primary text before it is used in any published figure or claim.
Many publisher sites (ScienceDirect, Wiley, ACS, GeoScienceWorld) blocked the
automated fetch; those items are flagged rather than filled from memory.

Frontier's pathway note (https://frontierclimate.com/writing/surficial-mineralization)
states, without citation: target cost under $80/t; 1 GtCO2/yr needs about 4 Gt
rock/yr "assuming 25% of the rock reacts with CO2"; Quebec holds 800 Mt of
serpentinite already mined (Thetford Mines).

## 3. Carbonation rates and uptake per tonne

Stoichiometric maxima (recomputed from molar masses; consistent with values
commonly attributed to Power et al. 2013, attribution unverified): serpentine
~0.48 tCO2/t, forsterite ~0.63, brucite ~0.76.

Observed passive rates:
- Baptiste (BC) lab columns: 3.5 kg CO2/m2/yr declining to ~0 after 60 days;
  0.6 wt% brucite fully carbonated in ~120 days. Power, Dipple, Bradshaw,
  Harrison (2020) IJGGC 94:102895, doi:10.1016/j.ijggc.2019.102895. VERIFIED.
  Quote: "little reaction of forsterite or serpentine was simulated due to low
  dissolution rates of these phases".
- Mount Keith: ~39,800 t CO2/yr (Wilson et al. 2014). VERIFIED.
- Black Lake: ~100 t CO2/yr passive on 110 Mt (Nowamooz et al. 2018). VERIFIED.
- Clinton Creek: ~6,300 t CO2/yr average over 26 yr on 10 Mt (Wilson 2009). VERIFIED.
- Woodsreef: 229-405 g CO2/m2/yr (Turvey et al. 2018 IJGGC 79:38,
  doi:10.1016/j.ijggc.2018.09.015, VERIFIED abstract); 11.7 kg CO2/m2 cumulative
  in the upper 1 m (Hamilton et al. 2020 J. Geochem. Explor. 220:106655,
  doi:10.1016/j.gexplo.2020.106655, VERIFIED abstract). Oskierski et al. 2013
  figures UNVERIFIED.
- Harrison, Power, Dipple (2013) ES&T 47:126, doi:10.1021/es3012854 (VERIFIED,
  full text): passive rates at Mount Keith and Black Lake "~56 [kt/year] and
  ~0.6 kt/year"; passive rates span ~1e-4 to 1e-2 tCO2 per t tailings per yr.

Enhanced rates:
- Baptiste: aeration 19 kg CO2/m2/yr (~5x passive); 10% CO2 sparging up to
  210 kt CO2/yr. Power et al. 2020. VERIFIED.
- Brucite: rate rises ~2,400x from 0.04% to 100% CO2; Mount Keith brucite could
  offset 22-57% of mine emissions. Harrison et al. 2013. VERIFIED.
- Hamilton et al. 2020 (Econ. Geol. 115(2):303) heap-leach: 4 weeks ~ 30 years
  passive; 21.2 kg CO2/m2/yr. UNVERIFIED (blocked).
- Kelemen et al. 2019: sparging "could accelerate the CO2 uptake rate up to a
  million times". VERIFIED.
- Woodall et al. 2019 GHG Sci. Technol. 9:1096, doi:10.1002/ghg.1940: no content
  accessible.
- Startups (Arca, Exterra): no independently verified removal rates; Arca's
  "200 g CO2 per kg tailings" is a target, not a result. UNVERIFIED.

Fraction of Mg carbonated in 1-10 years at ambient conditions: no paper states
it cleanly. Synthesis: passive removal is ~0.1-20 kg CO2 per tonne bulk tailings
per year, essentially all from brucite; bulk serpentine and olivine are
negligible on that timescale. NASEM 2019: "typical mine tailings with 3 wt%
labile Mg ... could capture and store about 21 wt% CO2 in 100 years" (VERIFIED).
Enhanced methods raise ambient rates by 5x to >100x.

## Cross-cutting flags

1. The 800 Mt Thetford figure is uncited inside its own primary source.
2. Passive carbonation varies ~2 orders of magnitude between sites of similar
   tonnage (Black Lake ~100 t/yr on 110 Mt vs Mount Keith ~40 kt/yr); brucite
   content, climate and pile geometry dominate. A tonnage-only potential map
   would mislead without a reactivity layer.
3. Load-bearing numbers still unverified: Thetford brucite %, Hamilton 2020
   heap-leach rates, Kelemen 2020, Renforth 2012 energy figures.
4. Startup pilot data: no independently verified rates.

## 6. Open copies found for the paywalled anchors (2026-09-08, second scan)

Author manuscripts and theses, legally open, were located for four of five items;
numbers below are quoted from those files.

- **Assima et al. 2012 (IJGGC 12:124) and 2013 (Thermochim. Acta 566:281)**: reproduced
  verbatim as chapters III and VI of Assima's 2014 Universite Laval PhD thesis
  (corpus.ulaval.ca/handle/20.500.11794/24881). Black Lake chrysotile residue: "Free
  brucite in CMR was estimated to amount to 2.94 wt.%"; 4-day CO2 uptake 6.5-15.5 mL
  CO2/g by size fraction, 93.7% of it from brucite; optimum liquid saturation
  "near beta ~ 30-35%". Table VI.5 brucite wt%: Black Lake <75 um 3.914, Asbestos
  <75 um 6.442, Raglan 0.000, Dumont 10.883, Renard 1.759. VERIFIED PRIMARY.
- **Pronost et al. 2011 (ES&T 45:9413)**: no open copy; brucite "10-15 wt%" for Ni
  residues only as cited by Assima. NOT FOUND (secondary only).
- **Palandri & Kharaka 2004 (USGS OFR 2004-1068)**: PDF retrieved
  (pubs.usgs.gov/of/2004/1068/pdf/OFR_2004_1068.pdf); rows transcribed into
  constants.py (forsterite, enstatite, diopside, anorthite, lizardite, chrysotile,
  brucite, magnesite). Antigorite is absent from the report. VERIFIED PRIMARY.
- **Hamilton et al. 2020 (Econ. Geol. 115:303)**: pre-submission version is chapter 5
  of Hamilton's 2019 Monash thesis (10.4225/03/5b09f98e7230b). Direct CO2-gas
  columns: 20.1 and 16.1 g CO2/kg in 28 days, equal to "33 years ... of passive
  carbonation via air capture at the Woodsreef Mine (17.6 g CO2/kg)"; 261.1 and
  209.8 g CO2/kg/y. Heap leaching: "21.4 kg CO2/m2/y ... approximately 3.5 times
  the highest reported rate for passive carbonation in the field (Clinton Creek)";
  the results text says "21,414 g C/m2/y", an unreconciled unit discrepancy.
  Cementation noted as passivating. VERIFIED against the manuscript.
- **Wilson et al. 2014 (IJGGC 25:121)**: accepted manuscript at Waikato Research
  Commons (10289/8967). "11 Mt/yr of tailings deposited evenly over the ~16.6 km2
  area of TSF2"; hydromagnesite ~4 wt%/yr in the upper 10 cm; "~39,800 t CO2/yr";
  brucite "1.0-2.5 wt.%" at depth, 2.5 used as the model input. So the passive
  rate is ~3.6 kg CO2 per tonne of new tailings per year. VERIFIED PRIMARY.
- **Gras et al. 2020 (Chem. Geol. 546:119661)**: accepted manuscript at
  corpus.ulaval.ca (20.500.11794/39308). "1.4 (+/-0.3) kgCO2/tonne/year" over 4 yr
  in cell EC-2 (5 t, ~19 m2, 35 cm thick; EC-1 104 t, 2 m); Dumont brucite "10.2
  wt%" (elsewhere "10.2 vol%"), vs 10.88 in Assima 2013: unreconciled. Mine-scale
  projection ~21 kt CO2/yr on 15 Mt/yr tailings (~16% of emissions). VERIFIED.
- **Forced-air pilots**: Arca patent WO2025199640A1 confirms ambient air and 30-35
  day cycles but discloses no rate. Stubbs et al. is 2021 (IJGGC 113:103554),
  closed. Zeyen et al. 2022 is kimberlite smectite, theoretical only. Kelemen et
  al. 2018 Energy Procedia not retrievable. Exterra publishes no rates.

Implication for the model: at Dumont, 10.9 wt% brucite (f_max ~0.066 tCO2/t) yielded
1.4 kg/t/yr in a 35 cm cell in Quebec, i.e. ~8.5% of the brucite carbonated in 4
years; at Mount Keith 1-2.5 wt% brucite yields 3.6 kg/t/yr in the warm, actively
deposited upper 10 cm. Passive rates are supply- and exposure-limited, which is what
the aerated and irrigated reactor mode is meant to overcome.
